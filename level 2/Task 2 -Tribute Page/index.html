Humanitarian

A Life Dedicated to Others

Abdul Sattar Edhi was a Pakistani humanitarian and philanthropist who dedicated his life to helping people in need. Through simple beginnings and extraordinary determination, he developed services that supported the sick, homeless, abandoned children, elderly people and victims of emergencies.

He became widely respected because his humanitarian work focused on helping people regardless of their religion, background, social position or financial status.

“
My religion is humanitarianism, which is the basis of every religion in the world.

— Abdul Sattar Edhi

His Story

Biography

Abdul Sattar Edhi was born in 1928 in Bantva, in what was then British India. After the partition of the subcontinent in 1947, his family moved to Pakistan. He eventually settled in Karachi, where he witnessed poverty, illness and the lack of basic welfare services experienced by many people.

Instead of waiting for others to solve these problems, Edhi began helping people himself. His early welfare work started on a small scale, but public trust and donations gradually allowed his services to expand. During a major flu epidemic in 1957, his relief work increased significantly and he was able to acquire an ambulance.

His humanitarian activities eventually developed into the Edhi Foundation. Its services expanded to include ambulance operations, shelters, orphan care, medical assistance, rehabilitation centres and emergency support. His wife, Bilquis Edhi, also played an important role in expanding welfare services, particularly for women and abandoned children.

Despite receiving national and international recognition, Edhi was known for living very simply. He continued working closely with people in need instead of adopting a luxurious lifestyle. His life became an example of how compassion combined with practical action can create lasting social change.
